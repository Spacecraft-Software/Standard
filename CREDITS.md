# CREDITS

Third-party work that The Steelbore Standard substantially builds upon, per
§15.3. This file is the human-readable narrative counterpart to the mechanical
SPDX metadata every file in this repository carries under §4.3 — §15.2 tells
consumers who maintains Spacecraft Software, and this file tells them whose
work it stands on.

Nothing here is a dependency in the §24 sense; both entries are specifications
whose *principles* were imported while the standard was written.

## ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148:2018

*Systems and software engineering — Life cycle processes — Requirements
engineering.*

Informs **§20 — Requirements Engineering** and **§21 — Verification,
Validation & Traceability**, added in v2.00. Taken as principles: the
separation of stakeholder needs from a requirements specification; the nine
individual and five set characteristics a requirement is assessed against
(§20.4); the attribute set each requirement carries, including the
verification method chosen at authoring time; and the four admissible
verification methods of §21.1.

**No text is reproduced.** ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148:2018 is copyrighted and sold
under restrictive terms; this document is CC-BY-SA-4.0. Every clause in §20
and §21 is written in this standard's own words, and the characteristic lists
are restatements rather than quotations. Where 29148 assumes a
customer/supplier contract and a tree of deliverable documents, this standard
substitutes what a single maintainer at hobby pace can execute (§19).

Copyright ISO/IEC/IEEE. <https://www.iso.org/standard/72089.html>

## ECSS-E-ST-40C

*Space engineering: Software.* European Cooperation for Space Standardization.

Informs **§19 — Assurance Categories, Tailoring & Conformance**, **§21.4**,
**§22 — Resource Budgets & Margins**, **§25 — Baselines, Anomalies & Release
Acceptance**, and **§26 — Operations, Maintenance & Support**, added in v2.00.
Taken as principles: the criticality-category model and its graded
obligations; the recorded tailoring register that replaces an ad-hoc "not
applicable"; the staged review sequence, compressed to the three gates of
§19.4; the resource-budget and margin-band discipline of §22; the
consequence-based anomaly classification of §25; and the principle that
verification independence scales with criticality (§21.4).

**No text is reproduced.** ECSS standards are copyrighted by the European
Cooperation for Space Standardization. Where ECSS assumes an independent
verification organization, this standard obtains independence from a different
*mechanism* rather than a different person (§21.4) — the principles are
imported, the organizational structure is not.

Copyright ECSS / ESA. <https://ecss.nl/>
